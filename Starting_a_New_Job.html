B1 Grammar: Will vs Going To

Use will for quick decisions, promises and offers. Use going to for plans or intentions that already exist.

FormUseExample
will + verba quick decision, promise or offerI’ll be back in five minutes.
will + verbsomething decided nowI’ll introduce you to the team.
be going to + verba plan or intention already arrangedWe’re going to take you out for lunch.
be going to + verbsomething planned for laterYou’re going to complete the onboarding online.

Grammar Exercise 2: Choose the best form

Click each sentence to reveal the answer and reason.

I’m free now. I ____ show you your office.
I’ll show you your office. Use will for a quick offer or decision.
The team planned this earlier. We ____ take you out for lunch.
We’re going to take you out for lunch. Use going to for an existing plan.
Wait here. I ____ come back in ten minutes.
I’ll come back in ten minutes. Use will for a promise.
Today you ____ fill out the onboarding forms online.
Today you’re going to fill out the onboarding forms online. Use going to for a planned activity.
