Lesson Overview

Students watch a short presentation-skills video about speaking clearly. The lesson practises B1 language for giving information, using simple language, preparing a conversational presentation and checking audience understanding.

Before you watch

  1. What makes a presentation difficult to understand?
  2. Do you prefer simple language or technical language in a presentation?
  3. How can a speaker check if their presentation is clear?

Typical Presentation and Signposting Language

Use signposting language to guide your audience through your presentation. It helps listeners understand where you are, what comes next and why each point matters.

FunctionUseful languageExample
OpeningToday, I’m going to talk about...
The aim of this presentation is...		Today, I’m going to talk about how to make presentations clearer.
Introducing the structureThere are three main points.
First, I’ll explain... Then, I’ll look at...		There are three main points: language, tone and practice.
Moving to a new pointLet’s move on to...
Now, I’d like to talk about...		Let’s move on to conversational tone.
Giving examplesFor example,...
For instance,...		For example, you can practise with someone who does not know your topic.
EmphasisingThe key point is...
What’s important here is...		The key point is that your audience must understand the first time.
Checking understandingDoes that make sense?
Is that clear so far?		Is that clear so far?
ConcludingTo sum up,...
In conclusion,...		To sum up, use simple language, a conversational tone and practise first.
Inviting questionsAre there any questions?
I’d be happy to answer any questions.		I’d be happy to answer any questions.
